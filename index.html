Pattern analysis
8 patterns to know — tap to expand
Gaslighting
Made to doubt your own memory or sanity.
Manipulation
They deny events happened. You stop trusting yourself. Journalling exact words — immediately after — creates an external memory they cannot rewrite.
DARVO
You raise a concern. They become the victim.
Deflection
Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim & Offender. You confront something real; you end up apologising. Counter: "I notice we've moved from what I raised to how I raised it."
Love-bombing
Overwhelming affection — then withdrawal.
Control cycle
Intensity that feels intoxicating but doesn't match the relationship's actual safety. Creates dependency. It cycles — the warmth is real. So is what follows it.
Intermittent reinforcement
You never know which version you'll get.
Addiction pattern
Random hot-and-cold creates anxious attachment — neurologically similar to addiction. That's why you can't stop thinking about them. It is not love.
Triangulation
A third person brought in to create insecurity.
Control
Mentioning exes, comparing you to others. Creates competition where there should be security.
Coercive control
Your freedom is quietly, slowly narrowed.
Abuse pattern
Isolating from friends or family, monitoring, controlling finances — often framed as caring. Legally recognised as abuse in many countries.
Emotional drain
Every interaction leaves you exhausted.
Chronic stress
You walk on eggshells, monitor their mood, manage their emotions while yours are dismissed. Exhausting by design.
Moving the goalposts
You do what they asked. It's never enough.
Invalidation
Each time you meet a stated expectation, it changes. Creates permanent inadequacy. The goal is not the goal — the goal is your constant striving.
⚠️ If you feel physically unsafe — contact emergency services or a crisis line immediately.

Proven techniques
Grey rock method
Become uninteresting. No emotion to feed on.
When you can't cut contact but need to protect your energy. Narcissistic behaviour feeds on your reaction — remove it, remove the reward.
"Mm. Okay."
"I see."
"Sure, whatever works for you."
Broken record boundary
State it once. Repeat calmly. Never justify.
Each new reason becomes a new thing to argue with. Repeat the boundary identically when pushed.
"I won't be discussing this."
"That doesn't work for me."
"I've said what I have to say."
DARVO counter
Name the flip. Hold your ground.
Naming the move disrupts it. You don't need to explain the term.
"I notice we've moved from what I raised to how I raised it."
"We can talk about how I expressed it — but the issue still stands."
Disengagement without escalation
Leave a conversation going nowhere.
State it once. Don't ask permission. Don't explain further. Actually leave.
"I'm not able to have this conversation right now."
"I'm going to step away. We can come back to this."
Counter-gaslighting anchor
Hold your ground when your reality is being rewritten.
You don't need to convince them. You need to not be convinced by them.
"I know what I heard."
"We may remember it differently. I'm clear on what happened for me."

🤕 Head & brain High urgency
Tension headachesBrain fogMemory lossMigraines
Chronic cortisol literally shrinks the hippocampus, impairing memory and learning. Gaslighting compounds this by attacking your trust in your own recollection.

What helps: Journalling as an external memory. Most cognitive symptoms reverse after the stressor is removed.

See a doctor if: sudden severe headache, vision changes, or worsening cognition over weeks.
😬 Jaw & neck Moderate
TMJ disorderBruxism (grinding)Neck stiffness
The jaw and neck are primary stress-storage zones. Nocturnal teeth grinding is strongly linked to hypervigilance — the body stays on guard during sleep.

What helps: Dental night guard for bruxism. Physiotherapy for TMJ.

See a doctor if: jaw pain limits opening/closing, or persistent clicking sounds.
💔 Chest & heart See a doctor
PalpitationsChest tightnessHigh blood pressure
Chronic stress keeps the sympathetic nervous system activated continuously. Stress cardiomyopathy is a real, documented medical condition — not a metaphor.

See a doctor if: any chest pain, palpitations over a few minutes, or breathlessness — rule out cardiac causes first, always.
🫁 Digestive system High urgency
IBS symptomsNausea before conflictStomach knots
The gut-brain axis responds directly to cortisol. Many people are treated for IBS for years without the emotional cause being identified.

See a doctor if: blood in stool or significant unintentional weight loss.
💪 Muscles & body Moderate
Chronic fatigueWidespread painFlinching at sounds
Fibromyalgia has a documented association with trauma. The exaggerated startle response is a recognised trauma symptom — the nervous system is permanently on high alert.

What helps: Gentle movement. Somatic therapy. EMDR has strong evidence for physical trauma symptoms.
🛡️ Immune system Track over time
Frequent illnessSlow healingAutoimmune flares
Elevated cortisol directly suppresses immune function. Prolonged stress can trigger or worsen autoimmune conditions — lupus, RA, psoriasis, thyroid disorders.

See a doctor if: 3+ infections in 6 months, or suspected autoimmune symptoms.
⚡ Nervous system Seek support
Profound exhaustionDissociationEmotional numbness
After sustained high-alert states, the nervous system can enter a shutdown freeze response. Emotional numbness and dissociation are recognised trauma responses — not weakness.

What helps: EMDR, somatic therapy, or trauma-focused CBT. Professional support is needed.
🏋️ Shoulders & back Moderate
Shoulder tensionUpper back painPostural collapse
Chronic protective posturing — hunching, shoulders raised — is a documented trauma response. It creates muscle imbalances and nerve compression over time.

What helps: Physiotherapy and postural correction. Somatic therapy to release the protective pattern.
